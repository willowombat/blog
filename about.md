# About

I work in construction materials testing and quality control — the unglamorous but essential business of making sure things don't fall down. My days involve soils, concrete, and masonry, a fair amount of paperwork, and the occasional argument with a spec sheet.

Outside of work, I cook. Nothing precious — just an ongoing curiosity about what goes well with what and why, and a willingness to make the same dish a dozen times until it's right.

I have a long-standing interest in natural history and historical ecology: how ecosystems looked before we got to them, what lived where, and how much we've quietly normalized a diminished world. The passenger pigeon is a particular obsession.

I'm drawn to emerging technology in ways that are probably disproportionate to my formal background in it. Artificial intelligence, nanotechnology, and biohacking all occupy a significant amount of my reading time and mental real estate. I find the edges of biology and engineering genuinely exciting — not in a breathless futurist way, but in a *this is actually happening and most people aren't paying attention* kind of way.

My Spanish is a work in progress. It's been a work in progress for a while. I'm still at it.

This blog is a place to think out loud about whatever is in front of me — whether that's something technical, something historical, a recipe worth recording, or just an idea I wanted to get out of my head and onto a page.
