It has been a long journey of recovery for me since Joe died. I have managed to get better control of my emotions. I am now better capable of handling stress and the day to day. Still, every once in a while something throws me for a loop. Sometimes when I am driving, the realization hits me. He will never be navigating for me in the passenger seat again. Last night I dreamed that we were together in bed. He was shaking me with his arm. He told me to wake up. That had been our ritual for the last 13 years we were together. It felt so flawlessly familiar. I didn't question it. But as I was in the intermediate steps of awakening, I realized Joe was gone. For the briefest of instants, I thought maybe losing him to cancer was the bad dream. I was hoping that when I opened my eyes, he would be there. Maybe the last few years had just been the dream. As I opened my eyes, I could almost feel his arm reaching for me from his side of the bed. Then, my eyes opened fully, and I was alone. The dream was him still being with me. The reality was me being alone in bed in the middle of the night. There's nothing quite like starting your day with sobbing. On the bright side, getting the sobbing out in the wee hours of the morning helps. Then, carrying on your day makes you feel like a tough survivor. Now I just wish I could figure out the reason I am supposed to be surviving at all. Surviving is a poor substitute for living.
